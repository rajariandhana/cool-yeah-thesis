## 1.1 Background
[11 C]
There has been a huge rise in hybrid forms of interaction, where some of the audience is in-person at a physical site (or multiple sites), and some of the audience is online somewhere around the world. A current major limitation of hybrid formats based on user criticism is the inability to network and socially interact for those online. The physical divide between in-person attendees and online attendees is such that, in the absence of innovative or novel technological intervention, the smaller group of online attendees often end up having a “fly on the wall” experience, unable to connect with those in person and with no tangible or effective way to signal presence.

The Future of Meetings (TFOM) is a community comprises of scientists, researchers, educators and industry representatives connected with the same goal, in how future meetings and interactions may be optimized by leveraging technology for improved accessibility, inclusivity, and sustainability.

[11 C]
TFOM seeks to explore solutions to connecting a hybrid audience, starting from the assumption that some of the audience is physically in one location and some are online. Specifically, develop a technologically-driven solution that enables the two audiences to connect to each other and also supports the signalling of presence between the two groups.

Consider especially the purpose of such a connection - e.g. in a meeting or conference, then the attendees may want to connect to people who have shared interests or expertise, or they may be hoping to connect with people from particular backgrounds.

Seeking to find a viable solution for this problem, TFOM gathered a team that develops HereToo, an ice-breaking platform application for hybrid meeting. As an ice-breaking platform, participants have opportunities to network and get to know one another before deciding if they wish to further share contact information on their own. The website serves as the intermediate step between being complete strangers to considering the exchange of contact information. To expedite this ice-breaking process, the website matches users according to their shared interests and area of expertise. This way, users (regardless of their mode of
attendance) will have a common ground when conversing with one another. This eases most of the initial awkwardness and tension. Subsequently, they will be
grouped accordingly and put into breakout rooms where they participate in a Bingo networking game. This Bingo networking game provides multiple opportunities for users to converse with others that have similar interests and areas of expertise.

The platform sets in a hybrid form and requires a robust backend as the backbone of the application. It must be able to communicate between participants instantaneously in a real time manner to ensure a seamless communication.

## 1.2 Problem Statement
Based on the background above, this research aims to address the following problems:
1. How to design a backend system that supports real-time communication between participants.
2. How can a communication protocol be designed in accordance with the flow of the platform's ice-breaking Bingo networking game.
